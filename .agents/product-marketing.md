## Product Overview
**One-liner:** We design, build and run custom AI agents that take over whole back-office workflows, inside the tools a company already uses.
**What it does:** We audit a company's operations, pick the workflows where agents remove the most manual coordination, redesign those workflows around agents, deploy them in production on the existing stack, and keep improving them afterwards.
**Product category:** AI implementation / AI automation consultancy ("AI agents for operations").
**Product type:** Service (done-for-you implementation, plus ongoing optimisation).
**Business model:** Per-engagement proposal priced on workflow complexity, systems involved and deployment scope; ongoing optimisation after launch. [NEED: retainer vs. fixed fee, typical engagement size]

## Target Audience
**Target companies:** Mid-sized and enterprise companies with finance, revenue, procurement, compliance or customer-operations teams doing repetitive, rule-based work across several systems. [NEED: size band, industries, geography — the site ships in EN/ES/IT]
**Decision-makers:** COO, CFO, VP/Head of Operations, RevOps and Finance Ops leads; IT/security as technical influencer.
**Primary use case:** Hand an end-to-end operational workflow (e.g. AP exceptions, deal approvals, vendor onboarding) to AI agents.
**Jobs to be done:**
- Get more throughput from the same team without hiring
- Stop work stalling in queues, inboxes and hand-offs
- Adopt AI in operations without a multi-year platform migration
**Use cases:**
- Finance ops: AP/AR, reconciliation, close, cash forecasting
- Revenue ops: CRM hygiene, deal approvals, pipeline reporting, commissions
- Procurement: vendor onboarding, approvals, contracts, supplier follow-ups
- Compliance & risk: regulatory tracking, audit evidence, policy monitoring
- Customer ops: triage, order exceptions, account updates, escalations

## Problems & Pain Points
**Core problem:** People act as the glue between systems: matching records, chasing approvals, rebuilding reports.
**Why alternatives fall short:**
- Point automation tools handle one step and leave people moving work between the rest
- Large software platforms need long migrations before anything changes
- Generic AI assistants help individuals, not end-to-end processes
**What it costs them:** Backlogs, slow cycle times (close, deal approval, onboarding), hiring to keep up with volume. [NEED: real numbers from customers]
**Emotional tension:** Pressure to "do something with AI" without a clear, safe place to start.

## Objections
| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| "Is it secure?" | Agents act within existing permissions and security policies; every action is logged and auditable. |
| "We don't have time for a big project." | Around 20 hours of the client team's time across the project, mostly explaining today's process. |
| "How long until we see anything?" | First deployments typically take 6 to 12 weeks from discovery to production. |
| "What does it cost?" | Proposal after the discovery call, based on workflow complexity, systems and scope. |

**Anti-persona:** Companies wanting an off-the-shelf chatbot, a one-off prompt workshop, or with no repeatable processes yet.

## Switching Dynamics
**Push:** Backlogs, hiring freezes, manual hand-offs, pressure to show AI results.
**Pull:** Workflows that run end to end on the current stack, in weeks.
**Habit:** "We've always done it in spreadsheets and email"; existing RPA investments.
**Anxiety:** Data security, agents making mistakes, internal disruption, cost.
